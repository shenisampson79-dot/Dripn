This is a very sharp question — and you’re right to pause here, because urgency + pre-signup is where many products accidentally break trust.

I’ll answer in layers, not just a yes/no.

⸻

Short answer (principle first)

At pre-signup, you should never promise time-critical value that you can’t reliably deliver without commitment.

So:
	•	❌ Do not promise “results in 45 minutes” as a guarantee
	•	❌ Do not introduce urgency anxiety (“you might miss your moment”) before signup
	•	✅ Do position speed as a premium capability, not an entitlement

Pre-signup is about credibility, not responsiveness.

⸻

The real issue you’re balancing

You’re juggling 3 things:
	1.	Trust – don’t overpromise to anonymous users
	2.	Perceived intelligence – don’t look slow or dumb
	3.	Conversion – urgency can convert, but only if framed cleanly

The mistake would be to treat community voting like chat support.

⸻

The correct mental model

Community voting is not:

“Help me right now”

It is:

“Sanity-check my instincts”

That distinction matters.

⸻

Recommended pre-signup design (clean + honest)

When user taps “Second opinion” (pre-signup)

The stylist responds like this:

“I can get community input on this.
That usually takes 30–60 minutes.”

Then show two paths:

⸻

Option A — Free, slow, trust-safe

Button:
🕒 Get community input (no account)

Microcopy:

Results aren’t instant, but they’re unbiased.

This sets expectation correctly.

⸻

Option B — Urgent (conversion moment)

Button:
⚡ I need this quickly

This is the only place urgency appears.

Tap → transition to signup.

⸻

What happens if they choose “I need this quickly”

Now you have earned the right to say:

“Fast feedback requires an active audience.”

Then show:
	•	Create account
	•	Priority voting (explain, don’t oversell)

You’re not charging yet — you’re explaining why speed costs.

⸻

After signup (still no payment yet)

Give them:

“We’ll aim for responses within ~10 minutes.”

Important:
	•	Say “aim”, not “guarantee”
	•	Overdeliver whenever possible

Then you can later monetise:
	•	Instant votes
	•	Larger sample size
	•	Trusted voters

⸻

Why you should NOT offer <10 min results pre-signup

Even if you technically could.

1. It breaks perceived value

If anonymous users get fast results, speed feels cheap.

2. It creates operational pressure

You don’t want SLAs for users with no commitment.

3. It undermines AI authority

The AI should still feel confident without votes.

Community is a layer, not a crutch.

⸻

What the stylist should always do instead (key rule)

Before any voting delay, the stylist must say:

“Here’s what I would choose —
community input can validate or challenge this.”

This prevents:
	•	Decision paralysis
	•	Over-reliance on others
	•	Weak AI perception

⸻

Advanced nuance (this is important)

You can simulate speed without lying.

Examples:
	•	Show “Early votes coming in…”
	•	Show 3–5 quick reactions (not full voting)
	•	Label as “initial reactions”

But only:
	•	After signup, or
	•	As clearly partial feedback

Never fabricate urgency or consensus.

⸻

Bottom line (lock this in)

Pre-signup rules:
	•	No time guarantees
	•	No urgency anxiety
	•	Community = optional validation
	•	AI remains decisive

Post-signup:
	•	Speed becomes a capability
	•	Urgency becomes legitimate
	•	Conversion feels logical, not manipulative
