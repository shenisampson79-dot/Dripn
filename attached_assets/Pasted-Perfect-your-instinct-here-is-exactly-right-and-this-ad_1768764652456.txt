# 1️⃣ Final DFY Tier Design (crystal clear, non-gameable)

## DFY Lite — £19.99

**Positioning:** *“Solve this moment without building a wardrobe.”*

### What the user gets

* **3–5 core outfits**
* Built from a **small capsule**
* AI shows **variations & rotations** across 14 days
  (e.g. jacket on/off, shoe swaps, layering)
* One specific use case:

  * Holiday
  * Work week
  * Event run
* 14-day access window
* Outfits can be **saved as static cards**

### What they explicitly do NOT get

* No persistent wardrobe
* No item-level intelligence
* No future remixing
* No learning beyond this use case

### The perceived value

> “I won’t repeat outfits for two weeks — without thinking.”

That’s psychologically *huge* for £19.99.

---

## DFY Core — £39–£49

**Positioning:** *“Build a stylist who understands what you own.”*

### What the user gets

* Up to **30 individual items**
* Proper categorisation (tops, bottoms, layers, shoes, etc.)
* Persistent wardrobe saved forever
* Outfits saved **and reusable**
* **30 days of active styling** included
* After 30 days:

  * Wardrobe stays
  * Stylist goes dormant until subscription

---

### Why these two tiers can’t be confused

* Lite = rotation illusion
* Core = combinatorial intelligence

Even a smart user cannot “hack” Lite into Core.
