# 1️⃣ Exact “+” Button UX Flow (authoritative, fast, habit-forming)

## Placement

* **Large “+” button**, centre of bottom nav
* Always visible
* One-tap access

This visually reinforces:

> “When in doubt, ask me.”

---

## Step-by-step flow

### Step 1 — Tap “+”

Modal opens with **one sentence**:

> “What decision can I help you with?”

Options (cards, not text links):

* 🛍 Choosing what to buy
* 👕 What should I wear?
* 🎯 Outfit for an event
* 🤔 Quick sanity check

No social language. No “post”.

---

### Step 2 — Upload / Select

User can:

* Upload **1–3 images max**
* OR paste a product link
* OR choose from saved outfits (if they have a wardrobe)

Microcopy:

> “Two or three options is perfect.”

This prevents overload and keeps cost down.

---

### Step 3 — Context (optional, fast)

One optional text box:

> “Anything I should know?”

Quick chips:

* Comfort
* Versatility
* Budget
* Trendy vs timeless
* Work-appropriate

No long forms. No questionnaires.

---

### Step 4 — Stylist Decision (the moment of authority)

The stylist responds with:

* **One clear choice**
* Short reasoning (2–3 lines)
* Optional “what this says about you” framing

Example:

> “Go with Option B.
> It’ll work in more situations and won’t feel dated in six months.”

Buttons:

* 👍 That helps
* 🔁 Second opinion *(if available)*
* ⭐ Save

# 2️⃣ Stylist Response Templates (non-negotiable rules)

These templates are critical — they prevent the AI from becoming wishy-washy.

---

## Core response structure (always the same)

1. **Decision first**
2. **Reason second**
3. **Confidence reinforcement third**

---

### Template: Shopping decision

> “Choose Option A.
> It’s more versatile and you’ll wear it more often.
> The other option looks good, but it’s harder to justify long-term.”

---

### Template: Outfit choice

> “Wear Outfit 2.
> It balances effort and comfort better for this event.
> You’ll feel put together without feeling overdone.”

---

### Template: Sanity check

> “Your instinct is right.
> This works for the setting and doesn’t try too hard.”

---

### Forbidden language (hard rules)

* ❌ “It depends”
* ❌ “You could go either way”
* ❌ “Both are great”
* ❌ Long explanations

Authority > exhaustiveness.

# 3️⃣ Second Opinion Logic (without undermining authority)

This is subtle — done wrong, it destroys trust.

---

## Who gets second opinions?

* ❌ Free tier: **No**
* ✅ Paid Personal Stylist: **Yes**
* ❌ DFY Lite/Core alone: **No** (unless subscribed)

This protects subscription value.

---

## What “second opinion” actually is

⚠️ Important:
It is **NOT** a different stylist personality.
It is **NOT** the community (yet).

It is:

> The same stylist, reassessing with a stricter lens.

---

## Second opinion framing (copy)

When user taps “Second opinion”:

> “I’ll sanity-check this with a stricter filter.”

---

## Second opinion response rules

### Case 1: Stylist agrees

> “I stand by my first choice.
> The alternative is trendier, but less you.”

This **strengthens authority**.

---

### Case 2: Stylist changes mind (rare)

> “On second thought, Option B is the safer buy.
> It’ll integrate better with what you already wear.”

Admitting revision **increases trust** when rare.

---

## Hard constraint

* Only **one** second opinion per decision
* No looping

# 4️⃣ Abuse, Latency & Cost Stress-Test (founder-critical)

This feature *can* blow up costs if unmanaged — here’s how you control it.

---

## A. Abuse prevention

### Free tier limits

* 1 decision / day
* Max 2 images
* No second opinion
* No history

### Paid tier soft limits

* Unlimited decisions
* But:

  * 3 images max
  * No batch uploads
  * Cooldown if spam detected

---

## B. Latency expectations (set trust)

### Default SLA (shown subtly)

> “Usually replies in under a minute.”

Don’t promise seconds. Reliability > speed.

---

### If delayed

Stylist says:

> “Give me a moment — I want to get this right.”

Humanises delay.

---

## C. Cost control (AI + ops)

### Average decision cost (AI)

* Image + reasoning: ~£0.05–£0.15 per request (at scale)

Even with:

* 5 decisions/day/user

This is still very healthy margin.

### Why this feature scales well

* Short prompts
* Limited images
* No long memory writes
* No human-in-the-loop

This is **high retention, low marginal cost** — the holy grail.
